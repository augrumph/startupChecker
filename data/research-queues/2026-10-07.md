## P1 — LinkLane

**Sparse thesis:** AI-powered freight brokerage. Quote, book, and track loads in seconds.

**Mode:** LOCALIZE_BRAZIL  
**Area:** Supply Chain & Logistics

### Why it deserves deep research
- Brazil has a regulated and data-rich road-freight ecosystem (RNTRC, CIOT, freight values, carrier categories).
- The workflow naturally contains quoting, booking, tracking, documents and operational exceptions.
- A Brazil version could be materially different from a US freight-broker clone because regulatory, tax, toll, carrier and payment workflows are local.

### What can kill it
- Existing TMS/digital-freight platforms already solve enough of the workflow.
- Brokerage margin cannot absorb acquisition/operations costs.
- Carrier/shipper integrations are harder than the AI value created.
- Shippers will not delegate booking/pricing authority to an agent.

### First experiments
1. Interview 8–12 shippers/3PLs with meaningful monthly freight volume.
2. Reconstruct one complete quote→book→track→settle workflow.
3. Obtain 20 real historical loads and test automated quote/booking recommendation.
4. Ask for a paid pilot/deposit before building integrations.

## P1 — Rote

**Sparse thesis:** AI-native insurance department for auto body shops.

**Mode:** LOCALIZE_BRAZIL  
**Area:** Insurance / Auto repair operations

### Why it deserves deep research
- Brazilian auto partial-claim flow explicitly involves insurer notification, shop choice/accredited networks and prior repair authorization.
- This creates an operational interface between repair shops, insurers, customers, estimates, photos, authorizations and supplements.
- The thesis is narrower and potentially more monetizable than generic "AI for insurance".

### What can kill it
- The insurer, not the shop, controls enough of the workflow that shops cannot buy meaningful automation.
- Dominant estimating/claims systems already own the workflow.
- Integration/access to insurer portals becomes the real bottleneck.
- Small shops have low software WTP.

### First experiments
1. Shadow 5 body shops through 20 insured repairs.
2. Quantify admin minutes and delay days per insured repair.
3. Identify which actions can be automated without insurer partnership.
4. Charge one shop for a concierge/manual-first pilot.

## P2 — Memorable

**Sparse thesis:** reducing agent reasoning into a graph search problem.

**Mode:** GLOBAL_FROM_BRAZIL  
**Area:** AI Infrastructure & Developer Tools

### Why it deserves research
- Graph-guided/selective reasoning is an active technical direction for agents.
- If it creates a measurable reduction in tokens/latency while improving reliability, the economic value can be real.
- This is not a Brazil-localization thesis; the relevant market is global.

### Why it is not P1 yet
- AI-agent infrastructure is extremely crowded and moves quickly.
- "Graph reasoning" can be a technique rather than a durable product/category.
- The buyer and monetizable wedge are not clear from the tagline alone.
- Open-source frameworks or model improvements can erase the advantage.

### First experiments
1. Define the exact agent workload where graph search beats normal tool-use/reasoning.
2. Benchmark cost, latency and task success against 3 strong agent baselines.
3. Find 10 teams already spending enough on agent inference/retries to care.
4. Get design-partner commitment around a specific measured improvement.

## Policy

P1/P2 here means **where to spend research effort**, not overall startup quality.

No thesis receives V10 Thesis Score until payer, WTP, economics, alternatives and auditable evidence are populated.
